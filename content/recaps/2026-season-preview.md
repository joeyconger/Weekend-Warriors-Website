**1QB • Full PPR • 10 Teams**

Ten rosters, one league, and a real question every August: who's actually built to win this year. Here's the full team-by-team breakdown, ranked by 2026 outlook, with a look at how each team's future is shaping up too.

## 1. Eating W's — The Team Nobody Wants to Play

*QB: Josh Allen | RB: Gibbs, K. Walker III | WR: ASJB, Jefferson, Nabers | TE: McBride*

Josh Allen is a top-2 quarterback in any format, and that alone gives this team a weekly floor almost nobody else in the league can touch. Jahmyr Gibbs and Kenneth Walker give you two legitimate workhorse backs, with Gibbs turning into an absolute monster given his receiving role in full PPR. Amon-Ra St. Brown, Justin Jefferson, and Malik Nabers form a receiver room that could headline three separate playoff teams by themselves — exactly the kind of target volume this scoring format rewards most. Trey McBride rounds it out as a top-3 tight end. There isn't a single weak link in this starting lineup.

**Future outlook:** As if the current roster weren't enough, Eating W's still holds a full slate of future first-round picks. This isn't a rebuild-later team — they've simply stacked both timelines at once.

**Verdict:** The team to beat, every single week, with no ceiling in sight.

## 2. Lamar you serious? — One Elite QB, a Loaded Receiver Room

*QB: Lamar Jackson | RB: J. Taylor, J. Cook | WR: JSN, D. London, Flowers, T. Higgins*

In a single-QB league, owning the best dual-threat quarterback in fantasy is enormous, and Lamar's rushing floor makes him close to matchup-proof on a bad passing night. Jonathan Taylor and James Cook give this team a legitimately dangerous two-man backfield. But the real separator is the receiver room — Smith-Njigba, London, Flowers, and Higgins is as deep and target-rich a group as exists in this league, and full PPR only amplifies how much that volume is worth.

**Future outlook:** Middling draft capital and an aging tight end room (Likely, Andrews) mean this team isn't built to reload quickly if the current core ages out — this is a win-now roster more than a long-term one.

**Verdict:** A legitimate title contender as long as Lamar stays on the field, with health as the single biggest swing factor in the league.

## 3. Retard Hours — Still Loaded, Even Without the QB Depth

*QB: Burrow | RB: Hampton, Chase Brown | WR: Pickens, Olave, Burden | TE: Pitts*

The two-quarterback stockpile (Burrow and Caleb Williams) matters less in a single-QB league, but this roster doesn't need the crutch — Burrow alone is a proven, high-floor QB1. Omarion Hampton and Chase Brown are two legitimate backfields rather than committee guesswork, and Pickens, Olave, and rookie Luther Burden give this team real target-share depth at receiver. Tight end remains the one soft spot, with Kyle Pitts still chasing the hype that's followed him for years.

**Future outlook:** A real quarterback surplus (Williams as trade bait or insurance) plus a solid receiver pipeline gives this team optionality most contenders don't have — they can push chips in or pivot without much pain.

**Verdict:** The clearest #3 in the league, and the roster with the least downside risk outside the top two.

## 4. The Shit Slingers — The PPR Sleeper of the League

*QB: Prescott | RB: Bijan, CMC | WR: Puka Nacua*

Don't sleep on this roster in a full-PPR format. Bijan Robinson and Christian McCaffrey combine as one of the best receiving-back duos in fantasy, and CMC in particular turns into a genuine monster when every catch counts. Puka Nacua is a legitimate high-volume WR1 who thrives on target share alone. Dak Prescott is inconsistent but capable enough to not sink the lineup on a given week.

**Future outlook:** This is where the story turns — dead last in draft capital in the entire league, having mortgaged the future hard to build this core. When Bijan, CMC, or Nacua age out or get hurt, there's very little behind them to fall back on.

**Verdict:** Bijan, CMC, and Nacua alone are enough to win most weeks this season — just don't ask what this roster looks like in two years.

## 5. My Kittle Pony — Old, Proven, and Built for This Format

*QB: Hurts | RB: Achane, Henderson | WR: A.J. Brown, Adams | TE: Warren*

Jalen Hurts remains an elite fantasy QB on rushing production alone, arguably the safest floor at the position in the league. De'Von Achane and TreVeyon Henderson are dynamic receiving backs, exactly the profile full PPR rewards most. A.J. Brown and Davante Adams are still proven, high-target veterans even with the mileage climbing, and Tyler Warren has looked like a legitimate weekly TE1.

**Future outlook:** Bottom-3 in draft capital and an aging core across the board — this is a championship window that's open right now and closing fast, with not much of a plan for after it shuts.

**Verdict:** A real playoff team this season, built to win now because the future isn't going to be pretty.

## 6. Xmus Jaxon Flaxon-Waxon — Solid Everywhere, Spectacular Nowhere

*QB: Purdy | RB: Barkley, Tuten | WR: C. Lamb, D. Smith | TE: Loveland*

Saquon Barkley is a legitimate top-5 running back in any format, and Brock Purdy is a steady, above-average starter who won't win you a week by himself but won't lose you one either. CeeDee Lamb and DeVonta Smith are established, high-floor PPR receivers, and rookie Colston Loveland has already looked ahead of schedule at tight end. Nothing here screams league-winner, but nothing here is a liability either.

**Future outlook:** Top-3 draft capital in the league gives this quietly balanced roster real ammunition — this is the team most likely to make the loudest move at the trade deadline if they decide to push their chips in.

**Verdict:** A playoff contender nobody circles on the schedule, sitting on the assets to become a lot scarier if they choose to.

## 7. The Ward-en of the South — Weapons Looking for a Quarterback

*QB: Dart or Ward (unproven) | RB: Judkins, Etienne | WR: Chase, McMillan, Odunze | TE: Ferguson*

This is the most talented group of skill players in the league being dragged down by the single biggest question mark: an unresolved starting quarterback job. Jaxson Dart and Cam Ward are both unproven, and in a single-QB format, that uncertainty hits harder than it would almost anywhere else. Everything around it is loaded — Ja'Marr Chase is the best receiver in football, Tetairoa McMillan and Rome Odunze both have real breakout paths, and Judkins/Etienne give the run game juice.

**Future outlook:** Thin draft capital, having clearly traded future picks for this current core of stars. The bet here isn't on the future — it's on one of these young quarterbacks hitting sooner rather than later.

**Verdict:** The receiver room could carry this team to a playoff spot on volume alone, but until the QB situation sorts itself out, this is a weekly coin flip.

## 8. Dominate777 — The QB Stockpile Nobody Needed

*QB: Mahomes | RB: B. Irving, J. Jacobs | WR: N. Collins | TE: Fannin*

Patrick Mahomes still gives this team a genuine top-tier weekly floor at the position. But the strategy of collecting Bo Nix, C.J. Stroud, and Kyler Murray behind him has almost no value in a single-QB league — that's roster investment sitting completely dead on the bench every single week. Nico Collins is a proven WR1, and rookie Harold Fannin has flashed real upside at tight end, but Bucky Irving and Josh Jacobs is a backfield with no real difference-maker and thin depth behind it.

**Future outlook:** Middling draft capital, and a QB room that's more valuable in a trade to a Superflex league than it is sitting on this bench — expect this team to explore converting that surplus into help at running back.

**Verdict:** Competitive most weeks through the air, but paying the opportunity cost of a strategy built for the wrong format.

## 9. The Fantasy Football Team — Too Green to Trust Yet

*QB: Drake Maye | RB: J. Love | WR: M. Harrison Jr., T. Hunter | TE: Bowers*

Brock Bowers is the best tight end asset in football, and in full PPR his reception volume makes him one of the single most valuable non-QB players in this league. Drake Maye has real upside in year two. But this roster leans heavily on unproven, rookie-driven production — Jeremiyah Love as the lead back and Marvin Harrison Jr./Travis Hunter as the top receivers is a lot of "we'll see" for a single season, without much proven veteran depth as a backstop.

**Future outlook:** By far the most loaded draft capital in the league — future firsts from four different teams. This roster isn't really built for 2026 at all; it's built for the rookie classes still to come.

**Verdict:** Bowers alone keeps this team competitive on a given week, but the real payoff on this build is a year or two away.

## 10. AustinFreeman — Not Ready Yet

*QB: Herbert | RB: A. Jeanty, D. Swift | WR: C. Tate, Waddle | TE: T. Kraft*

Justin Herbert is a genuinely underrated starting quarterback, and Ashton Jeanty's rookie role has already looked every bit as good as advertised. Jaylen Waddle remains a proven, high-target receiver. But past those three names, this roster is thin on established production, and there isn't much of a safety net if anyone goes down.

**Future outlook:** Second-most loaded draft capital in the league, stacked with premium future firsts. This is a textbook "sell the roster, buy the future" build — the current record is the price being paid for a much better shot in a year or two.

**Verdict:** Currently the least talented roster in the league, but armed with the ammunition to fix that fastest.

## Overall 2026 Season Preview & Predictions

Once you weigh this season specifically — single quarterback, full PPR, proven production over upside — the picture sharpens quickly. Eating W's is the clear favorite, with a lineup that has no real question marks and a scoring format that only amplifies how good their receiver room is. Lamar you serious? and Retard Hours form the next tier, both built around a genuinely elite starting quarterback and deep, proven skill-position rooms.

The Shit Slingers are this preview's biggest riser — Bijan, CMC, and Nacua are about as good a PPR trio as exists anywhere in the league, even with the future mortgaged to build it. My Kittle Pony and Xmus Jaxon Flaxon-Waxon round out a credible playoff mix, one riding a closing veteran window, the other quietly sitting on the assets to get louder.

The Ward-en of the South is this year's biggest wildcard — the receiver room is arguably second-best in the league, completely undercut by an unresolved quarterback situation. Dominate777 is paying the price for a QB strategy that doesn't fit its own league format. And The Fantasy Football Team and AustinFreeman are both playing a longer game entirely, betting present-day standings for a war chest of future draft picks that could make either of them the team to fear in a year or two.
